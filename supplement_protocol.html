1. THE KROME RULE

Purpose over hype.

A supplement earns a place only when it has a clear job, acceptable risk, evidence-supported rationale, and does not replace a more important behavior.

Tier 1
Foundation

Strong practical value when a real need exists (e.g., Creatine, Whey).

Tier 2
Performance-Specific

Useful for certain events or training demands (e.g., Caffeine, Beta-alanine, Nitrate).

Tier 3
Situational

Mixed evidence or context dependent (e.g., Electrolytes, HMB, Tart Cherry).

Tier 4
Clinician-Guided

Use to correct a specific health/nutrition need (e.g., Vitamin D, B12, Iron, Zinc).

3. SPORT & GOAL PROTOCOLS

Core Principle: Use the minimum effective stack.

Power & Strength Sports
Strength / Sprint

Examples: Football, baseball power work, sprinting, Olympic lifting, powerlifting, throwing events

  • Foundation: Creatine 3–5 g/day; adequate daily protein; whey only when convenient
  • Pre-training option: Caffeine, starting at low end of evidence dosing, 15–60 min pre if tolerated
  • Situational: Beta-alanine when repeated high-intensity bouts are important; electrolytes for hot/high-sweat sessions
  • Optional: Citrulline for pump/blood-flow goals (mixed evidence)
  • Avoid: Stacking multiple stimulants, proprietary pre-workouts, dehydration strategies
Field & Court Sports
Intermittent High-Intensity

Examples: Soccer, basketball, lacrosse, hockey, tennis, volleyball

  • Foundation: Protein adequacy; creatine may support repeated sprint/high-intensity training; hydration
  • Pre-game option: Caffeine can help some intermittent long-duration performance; trial in training first
  • Situational: Beta-alanine for repeated intense efforts; electrolytes during prolonged/hot sessions; nitrate for conditioning blocks
  • Recovery: Protein feeding plus carbohydrate-rich food after demanding sessions
  • Avoid: Using supplements to compensate for low carbohydrate intake, poor sleep, or chronic under-fueling
Combat & Weight-Class Sports
Combat / Weight-Class

Examples: Wrestling, boxing, MMA, judo, grappling

  • Foundation: Protein adequacy; creatine can support training but may increase body water/scale weight
  • Pre-training option: Low-dose caffeine if tolerated and permitted
  • Situational: Electrolytes during high sweat losses; beta-alanine for repeated hard rounds
  • Critical Caution: Do not combine stimulant stacks with intentional dehydration. Weight cuts require qualified sport/medical oversight.
  • Avoid: Diuretics, laxatives, unregulated fat burners, extreme water manipulation
Bodybuilding / Physique
Hypertrophy / Composition

Examples: Resistance training, bodybuilding, physique, fitness competitions

  • Foundation: Creatine 3–5 g/day; daily protein target; whey for convenience
  • Training option: Caffeine before sessions if it improves training quality without harming sleep
  • Optional: Citrulline for pump; beta-alanine if sessions include sustained high-intensity work
  • Health layer: Omega-3 when diet is low in fatty fish; vitamin/mineral supplementation only when intake or status warrants
  • Contest caution: Do not rely on diuretics, stimulant fat burners, laxatives, or severe dehydration for appearance
  • Priority: Progressive resistance training, sufficient calories/protein, sleep and adherence
Fat Loss / Weight Management
Calorie Deficit
  • Foundation: Protein adequacy; whey can be a convenient low-effort protein source
  • Performance support: Creatine can help maintain strength/training quality even if scale weight rises slightly from water
  • Optional: Caffeine may support training performance and alertness, but it is not required for fat loss
  • Hydration: Electrolytes only when sweat losses/session conditions justify them
  • Avoid: Fat burners, detox products, laxatives, stimulant stacking, and using supplements in place of a calorie deficit
  • Reality check: Supplements do not override energy balance
Lean Muscle Gain
Hypertrophy / Surplus
  • Foundation: Creatine 3–5 g/day; sufficient total calories; adequate daily protein
  • Protein tool: Whey 20–40 g when needed to reach the daily target
  • Training option: Caffeine if it improves training output without harming sleep/appetite
  • Situational: Beta-alanine for high-density training; citrulline optional; HMB not a default for trained athletes
  • Health layer: Omega-3 or micronutrient supplements only when food intake/status supports the need
  • Priority: A controlled calorie surplus and progressive overload drive the result
Cardiovascular / Endurance
Aerobic / Long Duration

Examples: Distance running, cycling, rowing, triathlon, prolonged conditioning

  • Foundation: Adequate carbohydrate, hydration, electrolytes when indicated, and adequate protein
  • Performance option 1: Caffeine before key sessions/races if individually tolerated
  • Performance option 2: Beetroot / dietary nitrate 2.5–3 h before selected endurance sessions/events
  • Situational: Beta-alanine is more relevant to hard efforts of a few minutes than long steady-state endurance
  • Creatine note: Not a primary pure-endurance ergogenic aid, though it may support strength training within an endurance program

4. HEALTH & WELLNESS

Life-Stage Guidance
Children (School Age)
Food & Growth First

Food, growth, hydration, sleep and physical activity are the protocol. Performance-enhancing supplement stacks are not appropriate.

  • AAP Guidance: Young athletes improve through calories, fluids, training, conditioning and rest.
  • Vitamin D: NIH target for ages 1–13 is 15 mcg (600 IU)/day from food plus supplements if needed.
  • Food Checklist: Use produce for vitamins A/C/K, eggs/dairy for B vitamins and D, whole grains for B vitamins.
  • Avoid: Energy drinks, pre-workouts, caffeine powders, creatine stacks and weight-loss supplements.
Teens (Ages 14–18)
Adequacy & Caution

Keep the focus on adequate food, protein from meals, carbohydrates for sport, fluids, sleep and recovery.

  • AAP Notes: Most young athletes do not need supplements when diet is adequate.
  • Key Micronutrients: Vitamin D: 15 mcg (600 IU)/day; Vitamin B12: 2.4 mcg/day.
  • Hydration: Energy drinks are not sports hydration. Avoid stimulant pre-workouts and dry scooping.
  • Clinical Oversight: For restricted eating, rapid growth, or vegan patterns, consult a pediatrician/dietitian.
Adults (Ages 19–70)
Purposeful Protocol

Use food first, then supplement for a clear purpose: performance, convenience, documented low intake, or clinician need.

  • Strongest Adult Options: Creatine for strength/power, Caffeine for performance, Whey for convenience.
  • Vitamin D Target: 15 mcg (600 IU)/day for ages 19–70. Cover daily intake from food before isolating vitamins.
  • Safety Checks: Review medications, pregnancy, kidney/heart health, anxiety and sleep before supplementing.
Older Adults (Ages 71+)
Muscle & Bone Health

Priorities shift toward maintaining muscle, strength, bone health, adequate protein, hydration and correcting nutrient gaps.

  • Vitamin D Target: NIH recommends 20 mcg (800 IU)/day. Skin production declines with age.
  • Vitamin B12: Higher risk for deficiency due to reduced gastric absorption with aging.
  • Creatine & Muscle: Studied alongside resistance training for muscle retention; review with clinician if taking medications.

7. ATHLETE SAFETY

Third-party testing matters.

Competitive athletes should reduce contamination risk by choosing independently certified products (e.g. NSF Certified for Sport, Informed-Sport, Cologne List) and checking current anti-doping rules. Certification reduces risk but does not eliminate it.

Banned / High-Risk Substances

Avoid products containing or masking banned or hazardous compounds:

  • Anabolic steroids and prohormones
  • SARMs (Selective Androgen Receptor Modulators)
  • Pure anhydrous caffeine powder
  • Unregulated multi-ingredient fat burners
  • Diuretics, laxatives and extreme dehydration protocols
Safety Rules for Minors

Children and teens do not follow adult performance stacks in this guide:

  • Priority: Adequate food, hydration, sleep and structured training
  • No energy drinks, pre-workouts or weight-loss supplements
  • Micronutrients only under pediatric or dietitian guidance
  • Always check third-party testing for safety and heavy metal compliance
