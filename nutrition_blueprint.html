2. ENERGY BALANCE

Start with an Estimate
Mifflin-St Jeor REE Equations
Men REE
REE = (10 x kg) + (6.25 x cm) - (5 x age) + 5
Women REE
REE = (10 x kg) + (6.25 x cm) - (5 x age) - 161
TDEE Estimate
TDEE = REE x Activity Factor
Understanding Activity Factors

Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) accounts for your Resting Energy Expenditure (REE) multiplied by daily physical movement and structured exercise.

Important Rule:

Activity factors are estimates, not exact measurements. Always use real-world trends in body weight, hunger, recovery, and performance over 2–3 weeks to refine your starting number.

Activity Level Multiplier Starting Description
Low 1.2 Mostly sedentary, little structured training
Light 1.35 Some daily movement, roughly 1–3 sessions/week
Moderate 1.55 Regular movement, roughly 3–5 sessions/week
High 1.75 High daily movement and frequent demanding training
Very High 1.9 Heavy training plus highly active work or multiple sessions

3. MACRO MATH

Turn Calories into Grams
Protein
4 kcal / g

Essential for muscle tissue repair, enzyme synthesis, amino acids and elevated satiety.

Carbohydrate
4 kcal / g

Primary fuel source for muscle glycogen and higher-intensity athletic training sessions.

Fat
9 kcal / g

Crucial for essential fatty acids, hormone production, cell membrane function and vitamin absorption.

Illustrative Macro Ratio Frameworks

These are starting frameworks — not universal requirements. Choose based on training volume and preference.

Higher-Carb Performance
50C / 25P / 25F

High-volume training, field/court sport, endurance-heavy phases.

Balanced Training
40C / 30P / 30F

General resistance training, body composition and mixed activity.

Lower-Carb Preference
30C / 35P / 35F

People who prefer fewer starches while still keeping carbohydrate available.

5. WORKOUT NUTRITION

Pre-Workout Timing
2–4 h Before
Balanced Pre-Workout Meal

Carbohydrate + protein feeding. Moderate fat and fiber if tolerated well without digestion issues.

60–120 min Before
Targeted Snack or Small Meal

Smaller snack or meal emphasizing fast-digesting carbs plus some lean protein.

15–60 min Before
Quick Carb Top-Off

Optional simple carbohydrate (e.g., fruit, rice cake) when needed and tolerated for immediate energy.

Post-Workout Timing
0–2 h After
Recovery Protein & Carb Feeding

Quality protein feeding (25–40g). Carbohydrate intake scaled based on training volume and next session timing.

3–5 h After
Normal Daily Distribution

Continue normal protein distribution (3–5 distinct protein feedings) across the remainder of the day.

<4 h Recovery Phase
Rapid Turnaround Protocol

When another demanding training session is coming soon on the same day, rapid carbohydrate replenishment becomes more urgent.

Key Takeaway

No panic window: total daily intake and protein distribution matter more than racing to consume a shake within 30 minutes.

7. EVIDENCE NOTES

Nutrition Guidance, Not Nutrition Dogma

The Blueprint uses the Mifflin-St Jeor resting-energy equation, NIH/NIDDK energy-balance concepts, NIH sports-nutrition guidance, and evidence-based nutrient-timing principles. Macro percentage charts are explicitly illustrative, because there is no single macro ratio that fits every goal and training program.
